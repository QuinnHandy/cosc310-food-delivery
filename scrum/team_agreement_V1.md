## Part 2 — Team Agreement
1. Team Values
Our team is guided by four values:
Respect — we listen to each other and treat every idea as worth hearing.
Accountability — we do what we say we will do, and we admit it early when we cannot.
Reliability — we show up to meetings and meet our deadlines.
Fairness — work is split evenly and everyone gets credit for what they contribute.
2. Meetings
We will meet once a week for about one hour, plus extra meetings in the week before a deadline if needed.
If someone cannot attend, they must tell the team at least 24 hours in advance and send their progress update in the group chat before the meeting.
3. Communication
Our main tool is a group chat on Instagram for day-to-day discussion.
For urgent matters (same-day problems, a missed deadline, someone not showing up), we will text or call the person directly.
All important information — decisions, deadlines, file links, and meeting notes — gets posted in the group chat so there is one shared record. Nothing important is decided in a private one-on-one message.
All shared files live in one Google Drive or Github folder that everyone has access to.
4. Response Expectations
Team members respond to messages within 24 hours, and within 12 hours if a deadline is less than two days away.
If someone knows they will be unavailable (exams, work, travel, illness), they tell the team ahead of time and say when they will be back.
If a member misses the 24-hour window without warning, another member will contact them directly. If it happens twice, the team will raise it with that member in a meeting. If it continues after that, we will contact the instructor or TA.
5. Respect & Professional Conduct
Respectful behaviour means letting people finish their thoughts, criticizing the work and not the person, and taking feedback without getting defensive.
Unacceptable behaviour includes insults, sarcasm aimed at a teammate, ignoring someone’s messages, talking over people, taking credit for someone else’s work, and making decisions behind a teammate’s back.
To make sure everyone participates, we go around the group at each meeting so every member gives an update and has a chance to raise concerns before we move on.
6. Accountability
If a member does not meet the expectations in this agreement, we follow three steps:
First time: a teammate checks in privately, asks what happened, and offers help. The work is rescheduled with a new deadline.
Second time: the whole team discusses it at the next meeting and agrees on a written plan for the member to catch up.
Third time, or if a deliverable is put at risk: we contact the instructor or TA and provide our meeting notes and chat history.
7. Decision-Making
We try for consensus first — we discuss until everyone can live with the outcome.
If we cannot reach consensus within about 15 minutes of discussion, we vote. Majority wins (3–1 or 4–0).
If a vote ties 2–2, the member whose part of the project is most affected by the decision makes the final call.
Once a decision is made, everyone commits to it, even if they voted against it. Decisions are recorded in the group chat.
8. Conflict Resolution
We will work through conflicts in this order before involving the instructor or TA:
The two people involved talk directly, in person or on a call rather than over text.
If that does not work, the issue is brought to the full team at a meeting. Each person explains their side without interruption, then the team discusses solutions.
If the team is still split, we use the voting rule in Section 7 to decide and move on.
If the conflict is unresolved after that, or if someone feels disrespected or unsafe, we email the instructor or TA together and ask for help.
9. Anticipated Challenges
Scheduling conflicts — we all have different class, work, and commute schedules. We will share our availability in a shared calendar in week one and keep one fixed weekly time slot.
Missed meetings — with notes posted after every meeting, anyone who misses one can catch up without slowing the team down.
Missed deadlines — we set internal deadlines two days before the real Canvas deadline so there is time to fix problems.
Unequal contributions — tasks are assigned by name in the group chat at each meeting, so it is clear who owns what and who has fallen behind.
Poor communication — the 24-hour response rule and the "post it in the group chat" rule keep everyone on the same page.
Disagreements — we use the decision-making and conflict resolution steps above instead of letting issues build up.
Crunch periods — during midterms and finals we will plan lighter workloads and shift work to weeks with fewer exams.
